Background: boarding is not just a scheduling problem.

Many boarding studies assume passengers follow instructions independently. In practice, passengers may travel in groups and partially coordinate their movement.

Ideal assumption

Passengers follow instructions strictly and board independently.

but

More realistic behaviour

Families and companions may board together, wait for each other, or partially disrupt the planned sequence.

groupscoordinationless predictable

The study asks two connected questions.

Research Question 1
Which strategy is fastest under ideal conditions?

Measured mainly by Total Boarding Time under ideal social-group conditions.

Research Question 2
Does social-group behaviour change robustness?

Measured by how much each strategy changes across ideal, realistic, and extreme group scenarios.

The contribution is to compare strategy efficiency and test whether the observed ranking remains robust when passenger behaviour becomes less ideal.

Method: passenger-level boarding simulation.

The model represents each passenger as an agent moving through a simplified boarding statechart. Strategy rules determine boarding order, while social-group behaviour can cause passengers to board together.

Findings: strategy ranking remained stable, but robustness changed.

Finding 1
WilMA and Steffen were fastest under ideal conditions.

WilMA achieved about 1511 seconds, closely followed by Steffen at about 1515 seconds. Both substantially outperformed Random and row-based approaches.

Finding 2
Random outperformed row-based strategies in boarding time.

Although Random produced substantial shuffle delay, it still achieved shorter boarding time than Back-to-Front and Front-to-Back. This indicates that shuffle delay alone did not fully determine boarding efficiency.

Finding 3
Steffen showed the largest deterioration under increasing social-group behaviour.

Steffen increased from about 1515 to 1574 and 1616 seconds (+6.6%), while WilMA remained comparatively stable at 1511, 1567 and 1555 seconds (+2.9%).

Key insight: passenger behaviour changed strategy robustness more than overall effectiveness.

Discussion: efficiency is not enough.

Why this matters

Social-group formation mainly affected robustness rather than overall effectiveness. Strategies relying more strongly on strict passenger ordering became more sensitive to behavioural variation.

Main conclusion

Under the assumptions of the present model, WilMA provided the most favourable balance between boarding efficiency and behavioural robustness.

Limitations

Passenger coordination was represented through simplified social-group behaviour, and richer adaptive decision-making was not modelled.

Future work

Future simulations could incorporate richer passenger interaction, dynamic decision-making, and more constrained overhead luggage conditions.
